The Pickle

As I added more and more devices, I run into multitude of issues with em... pretty much everything. Common issues I already touched upon:

  • Solution is not private, all apps will require different cloud service.
  • Buy-in into one ecosystem is just too limiting in possibilities.
  • Internet connection is mostly a requirement to be able to control devices. Automations run from cloud (not always though) so my weak internet connection at that point in time resulted in lots of automation misses or even miss-fires.
  • At this point I have several different applications I need to jump between. Every now and then some of these log me off for no reason so I don't even get notifications. This part, amongst others, is very frustrating.
  • IoT devices being what they are (chatty little suckers), they slowly start to kripple my WLAN
  • Worst of all, other family memnbers starting to be anooyed if a switch does not work, light turning off or on on itself, notifications come and go as they please,...
So how to get out of this pickle?

The Rabbit Hole

With all that in mind, I started my all-in-one-place home automation reasearch with clear requirements for reliability (using the best devices for specific job), to stabilize my wired and wireless network, to have one dashboard with all my devices in one place, having had one place for all automations whcih will not conflict, having primarily local control instead of cloud or over-the-internet, limit number of energy hungry wifi devices and cover all corners of the house with most effective connection type (three stories hause with thick steel reinforced concrete walls). All those requirements call for multiple changes from IoT ecosystems, through all of networking, wireless coverage redesign, LAN redesign, power wiring redesign with the focus on redundancy and much more that I did not forsee at that time. The more I learned, the more I knew, the more changes I had to plan, more to implement, which created new set of problems to deal with and learn and reseach again.

This is when I stumbled onto Home Assistant, which launched me into a neverending cycle of research → implement → fail → pull hair → try again → fail again → finally win → just to start all over again. Needless to day my hair never recovered.

Fespite all, over the course of several years I mostly successfully:

  • Migrated from DECO mesh to more stable managed Ubiquiti AP network
  • Moved all IoT to their own VLAN
  • Adopted all devices into Home Assistant
    • migrated to ZHA or Zigbee2MQTT (~100 devices) for stability
    • migrated to Z-wave (~10) for range
    • migrated to Thread for tinkering mostly (less than 10 devices)
  • incorporated surveillance solution into HA
  • Re-wired my home to enable UPS backup of the whole network
  • Attached NAS to host specific services as well as DATA
  • Enabled the control of all interior lights + added control and the lights itself along the whole garden and outdoor
  • ...and so much more

And even though I feel the jurney just began, it is rewaring and I'm genuinely excited about how things are progressing and how much I'm learning on the way. I have a specific outcome in mind where I want to land eventually with this, but that will be for another article...
